Ethical & Practical Reflection Step 5
⚖️

Bias & Fairness

Dermatology image datasets are known to skew toward lighter skin tones, which can depress accuracy for darker skin and for under-represented conditions such as Vasculitis or Vascular Tumors that have far fewer training samples than common classes like Acne or Tinea. A model trained on this distribution risks confidently mis-classifying exactly the patients it has seen least — the opposite of where caution is most needed.

🔒

Privacy

Skin images are sensitive biometric and health data. Webcam capture in particular raises the stakes: frames may unintentionally include faces, backgrounds, or other identifying context. This prototype keeps inference local to the session and does not persist uploaded or captured images beyond what is needed to render a result.

🔍

Transparency

The system surfaces its full pipeline — enhancement method, normalization, feature type, model, and a Top-5 probability breakdown — rather than a single opaque label. The interface also distinguishes real model predictions from simulated demo output whenever a trained model file is unavailable, so users are never misled about the basis for a result.

📡

Live-Stream Deployment Risks

Real-time webcam classification of medical conditions invites misuse: unsupervised self-diagnosis, false reassurance from a wrong "benign" call, or false alarm from a wrong "cancer" call, and informal surveillance if a camera stream is repurposed to scan people without consent. Continuous inference can also amplify a single bad frame (motion blur, poor lighting) into a confidently wrong streamed result.

🛡️

Mitigation Strategies

  • Always present the model's confidence and Top-5 alternatives, never just the top label, so low-confidence calls are visibly uncertain rather than authoritative.
  • Label every prediction with its mode (real vs simulated) and the exact preprocessing/model configuration used, for full reproducibility and accountability.
  • Treat the tool as a decision-support / educational prototype only — add an explicit on-screen disclaimer that it is not a diagnostic device and does not replace a dermatologist.
  • Average predictions across several consecutive webcam frames before surfacing a "stable" result, reducing the impact of any single noisy frame.
  • Require explicit user consent before activating the camera, and stop the stream and discard frames immediately when the user clicks "Stop."
  • Before any real deployment, audit per-class performance (not just overall accuracy) to catch the kind of skewed, low-sample-class weaknesses described above.
